## Findings

### melt-defacement-has-no-versioned-substrate

- **Severity:** P0
- **Where:** `docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-ranking-charter.md:71-77` (adversarial obligations) and `:83` (proposition 3, "Reprove+Melt"); classification axes at `:35` and criteria at `:52-65`.
- **What:** Melt's contract (f-029) earns its place by being the *gentle* subtractive — "retirement built as defacement rather than deletion, with an explicit prohibition on `os.Remove`." That is a reversibility claim, and it is only true if the defaced material survives somewhere. In this runtime it does not, reliably. The five things a shortlist member must supply at `:71-77` are steelman, attack, Goodhart mode, losing pilot outcome, and redundancy — there is no "what does this write or deface, and what preserves its prior state" obligation anywhere in the charter, and no reversibility axis in the criteria at `:52-65` or the classification at `:35`. So the one candidate class whose entire merit is that it takes things *off* is never asked whether what it takes off can be put back.
- **Evidence:**
  - After f-050 the synthesis fixes the subtractive target as the **context-file channel**, not the mutations store ("Where the tournament actually stands", conclusion 2). Those files are `SKAFFEN.md, CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md` (`internal/contextfiles/contextfiles.go:22`), prepended to every future system prompt (`cmd/skaffen/main.go:840-843`).
  - The only production path that commits anything is `execGitCommit` (`internal/tui/commands.go:801-820`), reached exclusively by a human typing the `/commit` slash command. `internal/git` is imported by exactly one file — `internal/tui/app.go:33` — and `AutoCommit` (`internal/git/git.go:35`) and `Undo` (`:46`) have no other callers.
  - `runPrint` (`cmd/skaffen/main.go:208-240`) never constructs a `Git` at all. Headless Skaffen has no versioned substrate whatsoever.
  - So a Melt/Reprove firing between two human `/commit` invocations, or in `--mode print`, defaces a compounded claim with no recoverable prior state. Defacement-not-deletion buys nothing on an unversioned substrate; it is deletion with extra steps and a better name.
- **Failure scenario:** Reprove is implemented first (the synthesis's own conclusion 2 recommends exactly this, and `:48` invites it). Its trigger is mechanical — source-commit staleness — so it fires unattended across sessions, including headless ones. Six weeks in, someone tries to evaluate Reprove's own reversal condition ("did demotion remove claims that were still load-bearing?") and the pre-demotion text is gone: defaced in sessions that never reached a human `/commit`. The candidate that was chosen for being removable turns out to be the only irreversible thing shipped. The early symptom, visible while withdrawal is still cheap, is the **first Reprove firing in a session with no `/commit`** — cheap to log, and nothing currently logs it.
- **Suggestion:** One obligation added at `:71-77`: *"the store or channel this candidate writes to or defaces, and the mechanism that preserves the prior state."* Then require any subtractive candidate's contract to name a versioned substrate — either an automatic commit at fire time or a shadow store — as an admission condition, not as a later implementation detail.

### no-untended-upkeep-obligation-for-any-new-store

- **Severity:** P0
- **Where:** `docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-ranking-charter.md:71-77` (adversarial obligations) and `:88-98` (required output); the sole cost term is the single matrix cell at `:62`.
- **What:** Frontier, Ash, Register, Trace and Assay each add a durable store or a per-read filter over one. The charter asks for nine output artifacts and five per-member adversarial artifacts, and not one of them is a **per-cycle upkeep figure** or a statement of what breaks the first time nobody tends the thing. Cost enters exactly once, as a rating on `:62` that reads as build effort. That is quoting the price of a super while recommending a split.
- **Evidence:** the discipline every new store inherits is already visible in the incumbent:
  - `Store.Write` (`internal/mutations/store.go:26-52`) opens `O_APPEND|O_CREATE` and syncs. There is no rotation, no compaction, no retention rule, no GC anywhere in the package (`ls internal/mutations/`: aggregate, best, inspire, mutate, signal, store — no prune).
  - `readJSONL` (`internal/mutations/store.go:143-175`) scans the **entire** file on every read and, on any unmarshal error, executes a bare `continue` — a malformed line is dropped in silence, with no counter and no error surfaced.
  - The result is fed straight into every Orient prompt via `BestSummary` → `Store.Inspire` (`internal/mutations/inspire.go:20-40`).
  - Layer Frontier's contract on this — f-032's `if frontier.IsExhausted(fingerprint) { skip }` guard inside `Store.Inspire` — and the untended failure is **bidirectional and silent in both directions**: an exhaustion row that survives forever suppresses a lead class permanently (there is no re-open rule in the contract), and an exhaustion row lost to `continue` re-opens a lead and pays for it twice, which is the exact failure Frontier exists to prevent. Neither shows up as an error; both present as "the loop just stopped surfacing that kind of lead," which is indistinguishable from an empty field — and indistinguishable, in particular, from the Fallow/Survey 2×2's "genuinely empty field" cell.
- **Failure scenario:** Frontier ships. Month three, `~/.skaffen/mutations/*.jsonl` is large enough that a truncated write during an interrupted session leaves one bad line; `readJSONL` skips it; a fingerprint marked exhausted in week two silently becomes live again, and the loop re-probes and re-pays for a lead it already settled. Nobody notices, because the only observable is a rising token bill. Meanwhile fingerprints that *did* persist have never expired, so the S candidate's addressable frontier is monotonically shrinking. Both effects are attributed to whichever S candidate was implemented, not to the untended ledger.
- **Suggestion:** Add one required column to the matrix at `:92` and one line to `:71-77`: **standing obligation** — reads/writes per cycle, growth per session, who prunes it, and the single sentence "when nobody tends this for six months, X happens." Require it stated in per-cycle units, not implementation effort. Any candidate that cannot answer the last clause is not shortlist-ready.

### subtractive-class-champion-elects-a-stage

- **Severity:** P1
- **Where:** `docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-ranking-charter.md:37` ("Compare the strongest survivor from each class"), with `:91` and `:94` fixing the class-grouped output shape; `:83` states the proposition as "**Reprove+Melt**".
- **What:** `:37` is a sound anti-count rule for classes whose members are rivals. The subtractive class's members are not rivals — they are three stages of one act: Reprove is the *trigger* (staleness against source-commit state), Retire is the *state transition* (f-006, "status transition"), Melt is the *representation discipline* (f-029, deface not delete, with the read filter as the actual retirement). The charter's own proposition 3 already writes them with a plus sign, and the synthesis composes them explicitly ("Reprove is the strongest contract for it because its trigger is mechanical; Melt supplies the retention discipline"). Then `:37` and `:91` require the class to field one champion, which decomposes the only composite in the field.
- **Failure scenario:** Melt wins the subtractive slot on the reversibility taste that `:63`'s mnemonic criterion and every reviewer's instinct will award it, and `:48` names it first to implement. Skaffen ships a defacement API with no trigger — a maintenance act that fires only when a human remembers to fire it, in a runtime whose one existing human-triggered maintenance act (`/commit`, `internal/tui/commands.go:801-820`) is already the untended path in finding 1. It is used twice and then never again, and the "subtraction is the real gap" conclusion is scored as tried-and-failed. The mirror failure is worse: Reprove wins, ships a staleness trigger with no mandated write discipline, and the first implementation reaches for `os.Remove` because the prohibition lived in Melt's contract, which lost.
- **Suggestion:** Amend `:37` with one clause: *a class whose members compose (trigger / transition / representation) enters the comparison as a single composite entry, priced and steelmanned as the composite.* Cost: one sentence. It also stops the reviewer having to invent a tie-break between three things nobody would choose between.

### repair-mandate-selects-for-maximum-obligation

- **Severity:** P2
- **Where:** `docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-ranking-charter.md:32-34`.
- **What:** `:32` asks for Stress-test's "**strongest** non-redundant contract, or an explanation why it dissolves." Strongest, under every criterion in `:52-65`, means most distinct; and in this field distinctness is bought with new machinery — a new store, a new trigger, a new per-turn record. So the repair clause's optimand is monotone in standing obligation, and it is pointed at the candidate with the *least* evidence behind it: Stress-test's only prior filing was refuted (f-007, gate-delta claim) and it was never re-contracted. The anti-generation guard at `:34` ("do not introduce unrelated candidates") does not bind here, because a repaired Stress-test is nominally *related* — so the charter's only surviving generation licence is also its most expensive. `:33` has the same shape against a subsystem that already ships: Simulate must be shown "distinct from Sweep," and Sweep is `internal/experiment`'s live `ExpandMutations` machinery (`internal/experiment/mutation.go:59`), so "distinct" rewards whatever Simulate adds *on top of* working code.
- **Failure scenario:** The reviewer, doing exactly as instructed, steelmans Stress-test into a counterfactual-trial contract with its own store and its own trigger, and it enters the shortlist at `:91` with a strong distinctness rating and no history — competing on equal footing with candidates that have verified runtime deltas behind them. It is the beginner's second brood box: bought before the first is managed, argued for on paper, never worked.
- **Suggestion:** Invert the optimand in one word — "the **cheapest** contract that is non-redundant" — and make dissolution the default disposition with repair as the exception that must carry a per-cycle upkeep figure to be admitted. For `:33`, state the Sweep baseline as shipped and require Simulate's delta to be expressed against `ExpandMutations`, not against an abstraction.

## Verdict

The charter prices adoption in build effort and never in tending. That is survivable for policy candidates, which change a number, and structurally unsafe for the artifact/capability class, which leaves a store behind — and it is worst for the subtractive class, whose entire claim to gentleness ("deface, never delete") rests on a git backstop that only exists when a human types `/commit` in the TUI and does not exist at all headless. Two one-line amendments carry most of the fix: a standing-obligation column with an untended-failure sentence, and a composite-entry exception to the class-champion rule.
