# fd-decision-comparative-ranking-integrity — round 0

## Findings Index

- [P0] governability-inversion-preserved — the criteria re-import the gate that rewards emitting nothing, then step 4 ranks exactly the emit-nothing class, so the matrix orders candidates inversely to governability (§Comparison criteria)
- [P1] uncertainty-has-no-scale — "explicit uncertainty" names no rating vocabulary, no confidence vocabulary, no combination rule and no tie-break, so the five-way policy ranking is underdetermined (§Comparison criteria)
- [P1] verdict-coupling-unstated — three verdicts are declared separable with no rule linking them to the shortlist, the ranking, or the pilots, so a coherent-looking output can recommend one candidate and pilot two others (§Decision distinctions)
- [P2] marginal-value-double-counted — "marginal value over no-S" is a function of the other nine criteria and is scored as a tenth, double-weighting whatever the null already lost on (§Comparison criteria)
- [P2] reversal-conditions-unmoored-from-pilots — reversal evidence is required but never required to be observable in the pilots the same charter specifies (§Comparison criteria / §Required output 9)

## Findings

### governability-inversion-preserved

- **Severity:** P0
- **Where:** `docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-ranking-charter.md:35` ("Apply the hard gates from the v2 charter"), `:56-65` (the ten criteria), `:36-37` (rank the five policy claimants; compare strongest survivor per class).
- **What:** The prior run established that the epistemic-separation hard gate grades *products*, so a candidate that emits nothing clears it most easily — and the charter re-imports that gate verbatim, adds no governability criterion, adopts none of the named repair, and then makes the emit-nothing class the subject of its only mandated ranking.
- **Evidence:** Synthesis §1, f-042 (heat 27, required fusion 1): "The epistemic-separation hard gate grades **products**, so a candidate whose output is an allocation change clears it by emitting nothing — **the gate ranks candidates in inverse order of governability.** … Fix: the **Warrant** contract plus a fourth gate clause." The charter imports the gates by reference at `:35` without the fourth clause; criterion 2 at `:57` is "epistemic safety and material separation," which is the same product-shaped test restated as a score; and no criterion in `:56-65` asks who authorized a spend or whether the allocation change is readable after it lapses. Step 4 at `:36` then ranks Fallow ("an S whose entire output is an allocation change and a single number"), Slacken (clock-only arm), Stint (an operation "whose only output is a held allocation"), Season and Divert — five candidates defined by not emitting. Step 5 at `:37` compares the strongest survivor per class, which propagates each class's winner rather than correcting the inversion inside the policy class. The inversion is not hypothetical in this codebase: `shouldEscalate()` turns ungraded counters into a model upgrade and `internal/router/costrouter.go:74-75` calls `tracker.reset()` *at the moment of escalation*, destroying the estimate that authorized the spend. **Failure scenario:** Fallow and Slacken score top-of-column on `:57` because they have nothing to separate; Warrant (`f-042`, the one capability whose whole point is a graded expiring licence) sits in the capability class and loses step 5 to Assay or Reprove on generativity; the charter's own required-output 6 then names a policy winner that installs an unwarranted allocation channel of exactly the shape f-042 indicts. Counterweight check: `:26` ("material epistemic separation is safer than annotations") is about the carrier, not about emitting nothing; `:76` ("failure/Goodhart mode") is a per-candidate narrative that never enters a score. No counterweight exists.
- **Suggestion:** One added criterion at `:65`: "governability of the change — for a candidate whose product is an allocation rather than an artifact, whether the authorizing estimate, the issuing authority, and the licence's expiry survive the change and are readable afterward (f-042)." Optionally note at `:35` that the imported gate's epistemic-separation clause is satisfied vacuously by non-emitters and must be read together with this criterion.

### uncertainty-has-no-scale

- **Severity:** P1
- **Where:** `:54` ("Use qualitative ratings with explicit uncertainty"), `:92` ("Qualitative comparison matrix with uncertainty"), `:94` ("Policy-class ranking").
- **What:** No rating vocabulary, no confidence vocabulary, no rule combining a strong rating with weak confidence, no statement of what a cell means when the underlying finding is `raw` rather than verified, and no declared tie-break for the total order step 4 demands over five claimants.
- **Evidence:** A working precedent exists in the source run and is not reused: `heat-ledger.jsonl` scores `novelty` 0–3 and `risk` as `{blast_radius, likelihood, product}`, and the synthesis moves scores on the record with stated reasons ("I moved f-031's novelty 3→2"; "I raised f-044's likelihood 2→3 because the default is in the code"). The charter inherits none of that structure. Concretely, walking three named claimants through criterion `:65`: **Fallow** — high marginal value, but its origin f-063 is `status: raw`, ledger likelihood 2, and its buildability depends on the refutation of f-004. **Stint** — moderate marginal value, origin f-067 `raw`, likelihood 2, and its predecessor f-052 was refuted. **Slacken** — high marginal value on f-071's compaction argument, `raw`, likelihood 2. Under `:54` all three are written "high / uncertain," "moderate / confident," "high / uncertain" and the charter provides no rule that orders them. **Failure scenario:** two competent reviewers run this charter on the identical verified findings and produce different policy-class rankings — one leads with Fallow on value, the other with Stint on confidence — and neither has violated a clause. Required output 5 then ships a ranking that cannot be reproduced or contested.
- **Suggestion:** One clause at `:54`: "Rate each cell on {strong / moderate / weak / not-assessable} and attach a confidence on {high / medium / low}. Confidence is capped by the disposition of the candidate's origin finding — a `raw` or `confirmed-narrowed` origin caps confidence at medium. Where two candidates tie on ratings, the higher-confidence candidate ranks first; where confidence also ties, the lower implementation cost ranks first."

### verdict-coupling-unstated

- **Severity:** P1
- **Where:** `:44-50` (§Decision distinctions, three verdicts, "Do not force the same winner across these questions"), against `:36` (policy-class ranking), `:37` (cross-class comparison) and `:40` (pilots for "the top two actionable designs").
- **What:** Separability is granted but never constrained. Nothing states which verdict the shortlist feeds, whether the "first implementation" winner must have passed either of the other two verdicts, or whether the "top two actionable designs" in step 8 are the first-implementation verdict's output or an independently chosen pair.
- **Evidence:** The v2 charter's lesson 3 (`docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-tournament-v2.md:35`) licenses semantic/runtime divergence — "It may pass one and fail the other" — but says nothing about the third verdict, which the ranking charter newly introduces at `:48`. The source run makes the incoherence concrete rather than theoretical: synthesis §"Where the tournament actually stands" recommends (2) "the one uncontested capability gap is subtraction … Reprove (f-030) is the strongest contract" — a *capability*-class candidate — while (4) says "The cheapest first pilot is **Fallow** (f-063)" — a *policy*-class candidate — and step 4 of the charter ranks a policy field containing neither Reprove nor Melt. A run can therefore satisfy required outputs 5, 6 and 7 simultaneously by ranking Fallow first in the policy class, naming Reprove as the first implementation, and piloting Fallow and Survey, with no clause making that combination wrong. **Failure scenario:** the implementer receives a recommendation to build Reprove first, backed by pilots that measured Fallow and Survey and by a ranking that ordered five candidates none of which is Reprove — three internally valid artifacts that jointly justify nothing, and no reversal condition attaches to the recommended candidate because it was never piloted.
- **Suggestion:** One sentence after `:50`: "The three verdicts may name different candidates, but the first-implementation verdict must name a candidate that appears in the shortlist and whose pilot is one of the two specified in step 8; if the cheapest null-resolving pilot is not the first-implementation candidate, say so explicitly and state which pilot's outcome would change the recommendation."

### marginal-value-double-counted

- **Severity:** P2
- **Where:** `:65` ("marginal value over no-S") within the ten criteria at `:56-65`; also `:62` ("cognitive and implementation cost") against `:39`.
- **What:** Two independence defects in one list. (a) "Marginal value over no-S" is not a criterion alongside the other nine — it is the aggregate of them measured against a particular comparator, so evidence that already scored on generativity, falsification and pace is banked a second time, and any candidate strong on the substantive criteria receives an automatic tenth boost. (b) `:62` fuses cognitive cost and implementation cost into one cell while `:39` separately demands cognitive cost be "exercised explicitly" as a neglected dimension — so the requirement at `:39` is dischargeable by prose that never enters the ordering.
- **Evidence:** For (b) the source run is unambiguous that the two halves behave differently: synthesis Caveat 7, "Regions never reached: the human-factors/cognitive-load position (the charter's 'cognitive cost' criterion at :113 was scored by nobody)" — while implementation cost was scored throughout (e.g. f-063 "with zero new instrumentation," f-066's "one-hunk fix"). Bundled with implementation cost, cognitive cost will again be absorbed by the half that has evidence. For (a), note that `:36` already frames the entire policy ranking as "against each other and no-S" and proposition 6 at `:86` re-tests the null a third time, so the comparator enters the framework at three levels with no statement of which is the scoring one. **Failure scenario over weeks:** Fallow — whose defining property *is* its relation to the null — collects credit on generativity, falsification, marginal value, the `:36` ranking frame and proposition 6, all from the same single argument; the resulting ordering looks like convergent evidence and is one finding restated five times.
- **Suggestion:** At `:65`, replace the bullet with "marginal value over no-S — **derived**, not scored independently: state it as the summary of the nine criteria against the null, and do not enter it as a tenth column." At `:62`, split into "implementation cost" and "cognitive cost for a human operator" so that `:39`'s neglected-dimension requirement lands in a scored cell.

### reversal-conditions-unmoored-from-pilots

- **Severity:** P2
- **Where:** `:67` ("A recommendation must state what evidence would reverse it") and `:98` ("Confidence and reversal conditions"), against `:40` and `:96` (the pilots).
- **What:** Reversal conditions are required of the recommendation but nothing requires them to be observable in the pilots the same charter specifies, so the recommendation can ship with criteria no planned measurement could trip.
- **Evidence:** The source run supplies both a positive and a negative example, showing the distinction is real and unenforced here. Positive: f-063's losing condition — "if spontaneous exploration already meets the proposed quota, :43 resolves for the null **and takes every S candidate with it**" — is measurable "from existing evidence `tool_calls` with zero new instrumentation." Negative: f-065 establishes that the outcome channel does not record the failure mode the v2 charter's blind-score list depends on — the quality store's entry condition is a human reaching Compound (`internal/agent/agent.go:246-252` gates the `mutations.Aggregate` → `WriteForType` write on `phase == tool.PhaseCompound`), so sessions abandoned in an exploration rabbit hole leave no row. A reversal condition phrased "reverse if the treatment arm shows more unnecessary exploration on no-benefit cases" is therefore untrippable by construction. **Failure scenario:** required output 9 ships three reversal conditions, two of which read on channels that record nothing; six months later the design is not reversed, and the absence of contrary evidence is read as confirmation.
- **Suggestion:** Extend `:67` to: "A recommendation must state what evidence would reverse it, and each reversal condition must name the pilot arm and the specific recorded channel whose reading would trip it; a reversal condition with no such channel is listed under residual human judgments instead."

## Verdict

The charter's decision machinery is well-scoped but under-specified in exactly the places where a comparison framework produces its answer through its own structure: the criteria list re-imports a gate the prior run proved ranks candidates inversely to governability and then applies it to the emit-nothing class; "explicit uncertainty" has no scale, vocabulary, or tie-break behind it; and the three verdicts are declared separable without a single clause linking them to the shortlist or the pilots. It does correctly anticipate one bias — `:100` ("Prefer the smallest coherent architecture over preserving the acronym") is the counterweight f-041 asked for against the letter S re-entering at scoring time through `:63`, and that is worth preserving as written. Four one-line insertions — a governability criterion, a rating-plus-confidence scale with a tie-break, a verdict-to-pilot coupling sentence, and a "derived, not scored" note on marginal value — would make two independent reviewers converge on the same ordering.
