## 1. Novelty × Risk Frontier

The strict Pareto front is degenerate: three findings sit at the joint maximum (novelty 3, risk 9) and dominate every other row. The front is therefore reported as a corner plus its two arms, which is where the shape actually lives.

### The corner — novelty 3, risk 9

**`f-031` — main leads the last publish 42% of the time on the compared roots, so `clavain-deployed` fails on roughly two days in five for an ordinary reason, and STANDING is what makes that survivable.** *(synthesis-authored; the fusion `fd-releaseeng-supplychain-drift` × `fd-apiary-queenright` that the loop never attempted, with a number attached)*

Risk: blast 3 × likelihood 3. Release engineering's axiom is "there is always a lag between a commit landing and a deployment mechanism propagating it; a drift check that doesn't account for that lag will alarm on every ordinary release cycle." Apiary's is "robbing looks like prosperity — a mechanism that fires often is not thereby working." Neither lens measured the lag, and Question 2 asks for exactly that measurement. I measured it: over the last 26.2 days, 38 commits on `origin/main`, of which 8 are version bumps, seven distinct episodes where a commit touching the compared roots landed without a republish following it, **cumulatively 267 hours — 42% of the window — in which `origin/main` led the last published cache.** A daily check with fail-on-drift is therefore red about two days in five, in a completely healthy repository, for a reason that has nothing to do with a dead hook.

This inverts the run's consensus. Every finding in `c-standing-downgrade-defeats-fail` argues the check will go too quiet. The measurement says it goes too *loud* first — and then STANDING silences it, at which point the reader has already been trained that warn/STANDING is the check's resting state. The two halves compose into the worst available outcome: noisy enough to be tuned out, then quiet by construction when a real drift arrives. The plan's own justification at line 74 ("an unchanged drift becomes warn + STANDING under mk-rd1x and a grown one fails again") reads, against this number, as a description of the failure rather than a defense.

The recommendation this forces is the opposite of what the ledger argues. **Do not remove STANDING.** Remove the *cause* of the routine red: gate the content comparison on the publish contract rather than on `origin/main`'s tip. Compare the cache against the newest commit that bumped `.claude-plugin/plugin.json`'s version, and report "main leads the last publish by N commits" as detail, not as a finding. Then a fail means what the plan wants it to mean — the published version and the installed cache disagree — and the 42% goes to zero without weakening anything. Taste: **-2, `alarm-priced-without-measuring-its-base-rate`.**

**`f-013` — seven bare `os.Getenv("CLAUDE_SESSION_ID")` sites in `cmd/clavain-cli` survived W2 and W3 completely, and W3 could not catch them even if `cmd/` were scanned.** *(`fd-collation-conformance`)*

Risk: blast 3 × likelihood 3. I verified all seven independently: `phase.go:267`, `claim.go:206`, `claim.go:319`, `claim.go:366`, `budget.go:585`, `evidence.go:150`, `init.go:128`. None has a `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` fallback. `sideband.go:19-23`, in the same package, already implements the correct two-register chain with a comment citing `Sylveste-23k` — so the fix was known to the author and applied to one call site in eight. This is live code: `~/.local/bin/clavain-cli` resolves to `bin/clavain-cli`, a shim over the platform Go binary; `hooks/lib-recovery.sh:183` calls `bead-release`; `commands/work.md:86` calls `bead-heartbeat` on every task iteration. The state at stake is claim, release, heartbeat, evidence and billing attribution. W3's `READ` regex is `\$\{?CLAUDE_SESSION_ID\b|env\.CLAUDE_SESSION_ID\b`; Go's `os.Getenv` cannot match it under any circumstance, and `SCANNED_DIRS` is `("hooks", "scripts", "commands", "skills", "agents")`.

The composition with the other half of the plan is what makes this the emblem of the whole review: `bin/` **is** a compared root, so W5 would hash that binary against `origin/main`, find it byte-identical, and report green — on a binary that carries the exact defect the initiative exists to eliminate. Byte-identity to a published HEAD is not correctness when the HEAD is what is wrong.

**`f-029` — `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` in a child session carries the parent's id, and there is a fourth register saying so that the plan's table does not contain.** *(synthesis-authored; predicted by `fd-distsys-session-identity` axiom 5, exercised by no round)*

Risk: blast 3 × likelihood 3. The plan's Why lists four ways a session lacks `CLAUDE_SESSION_ID`: the mk-i3u8 plugin-cache loop, a hook timeout, `claude -p`, a runtime that never ran the hook. All four are exceptional. I ran `env` in this subagent, on the machine the plan targets: `CLAUDE_SESSION_ID` **absent**, `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` **present and equal to `45f5fb3c-9968…`** — the id of the session that spawned me and authored the plan — beside `CLAUDE_CODE_CHILD_SESSION=1`. Two consequences with no slot in the design.

First, A1 asserts that when both registers are set they name the same session. In a child they are not both set, and the one that is names something else. Every env-chain reader in a subagent — which is every `clavain-cli` invocation from a subagent's Bash tool, and every library function W2 routed through the chain — attributes to the parent. Whether that is a bug depends on intent, and the honest reading is that it is right for some consumers and wrong for others: a bead claim arguably *should* roll up to the parent that owns the work, while `/tmp/interphase-bead-<id>` markers, throttle files and the companion-cache key should not, because two concurrent subagents then share one key. The plan states no intent for subagents at all, which is the actual defect.

Second, `CLAUDE_CODE_CHILD_SESSION` exists precisely to say "the id you are holding is not this process's own." It is a fourth register, it is set, and the plan's register table does not have a row for it. This also re-prices `f-013` upward: the bare Go reads collapse in every subagent, not only in the four exotic conditions the Why enumerates.

Severity for reference: `f-031` P1, `f-013` P0, `f-029` P0.

### Mid-novelty arm — novelty 2 and below, risk 9

**`f-014` — the compared surface omits `cmd/`, so the check compares one build output against another and never against its source.** *(`fd-collation-conformance`)* Risk: blast 3 × likelihood 3, novelty raised to 2. **`f-009` / `f-017` — mk-rd1x's STANDING mechanism converts the deliberate fail to warn on day two, permanently.** *(`fd-releaseeng-supplychain-drift`, `fd-chronometry-precedence`; joined by `fd-apiary-queenright`'s `f-028`)* Risk: blast 3 × likelihood 3, novelty 1 — the max-risk commodity, low novelty because Question 2 asks it outright. Folding counts into the signature is free from data already in hand. **But read this with `f-031`:** removing STANDING without fixing the 42% base rate replaces a check that goes quiet with one that fails two days in five. **`f-001` / `f-020` — the claim → heartbeat → release triad spans a boundary at which the id changes, so the writer and the releaser resolve different ids for one continuous session.** *(`fd-distsys-session-identity`, independently confirmed by `fd-staticanalysis-lint-evasion`)* Risk: blast 3 × likelihood 3, novelty 1 / 0. `bead-autoclaim.sh` writes `claimed_by` at PostToolUse; `session-end-release.sh` gates release on `_claimer == _our_session` at SessionEnd; `hooks.json` confirms the three hooks fire at genuinely different times. The premise is decent evidence — someone paid a code cost for it, gating `BD_ACTOR` on `startup` alone — but it is a comment, and one instrumented compaction would settle five findings and my highest-risk arm.

## 2. Top Fusions

**The loop attempted zero fusions and produced zero emergent findings.** No FUSE directive was issued in any round; round 1 spent its three slots on two DEEPEN and one STEER-WIDE. What follows is one fusion I performed at synthesis time and reported as `f-031` above, plus the second productive pair, plus two honest negatives.

**`fd-releaseeng-supplychain-drift` × `fd-apiary-queenright` — emergent, and it is the run's most consequential finding.** Parents `f-009`/`f-010` and `f-028`. Intersection justification: release engineering owns the axiom that a drift check ignoring propagation lag alarms on every ordinary release cycle; apiary owns the axiom that a mechanism which fires often is not thereby working and that a standing count nobody acts on is how the colony dies in autumn. Each parent had half the argument and neither had a number. Intersected and measured, they yield `f-031`: 42% of the last 26 days had `origin/main` ahead of the published cache on the compared roots, across seven episodes, so fail-on-drift is red about two days in five before any real defect exists, and STANDING is then the only thing making it liveable. Evidence: 38 commits, 8 version bumps, per-commit root-touch classification, cumulative 267.3 h exposure. That the loop clustered `f-009`, `f-017` and `f-028` together as "the check goes quiet" and never asked how often it goes loud is the single clearest cost of running zero fusion rounds.

**`fd-distsys-session-identity` × `fd-staticanalysis-lint-evasion` — emergent, not attempted, both parents touched one pattern from opposite sides without naming it.** `f-004` flags that `hooks/lib.sh` passes `""` as the stdin argument to opt out of the highest-priority register — correct at that call site, copyable as an idiom, invisible to W3. `f-026` notes that in Go, `phase.go:227` calls `writeBeadSideband("", …)` unconditionally. That is one anti-pattern in two languages: **an empty first argument that means both "no stdin exists here" and "skip stdin deliberately," with nothing at review time or in any grep that can tell them apart.** The fix is at the helper's altitude, not the call sites': a distinct sentinel, or a separate `clavain_session_id_env` entry point, so opting out is spelled differently from having nothing to pass. The controller clustered these apart (`c-discovery-cache-stdin-skip` versus `c-fix-cheaper-at-sideband-altitude`), which is why neither lens saw the other's half.

**`fd-collation-conformance` × `fd-distsys-session-identity` — independent here.** A genuine negative. Collation's own `failure_mode` says it "cannot judge whether a value is stale, only whether two artifacts differ"; distsys's says it is "not positioned to judge the git/hash mechanics of the rig check." Their strongest findings, `f-013` and `f-001`, share a variable name and no mechanism. Fusing them produces restatement, not intersection.

**`fd-chronometry-precedence` × `fd-apiary-queenright` — converged without fusing.** Chronometry's "an alarm that fires on schedule stops being read" and apiary's "a standing high count that has stopped being acted on is the ordinary way a colony dies in the autumn" are one axiom in two vocabularies. Both landed independently on `c-standing-downgrade-defeats-fail` alongside release engineering. Three unrelated lenses converging on one mechanism is the run's strongest confidence signal and it needed no fusion to get there — which is also why the *unasked* half of that mechanism stayed unasked for three rounds.

## Caveats

- **The highest-risk register cluster rests on a code comment.** `c-session-id-churn-compact-resume` (5 findings) is founded on `session-start.sh:31-36` saying "session_id may change on those." No round observed an id changing across a compaction, and neither did I. Those five findings carry `status: raw` in the surfaced set for exactly this reason: the evidence is documentary, not behavioral. One instrumented compaction settles it and should precede any W4 redesign.
- **`f-029`'s cause is ambiguous even though its effect is not.** I observed `CLAUDE_SESSION_ID` absent and `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` holding the spawning session's id in this subagent. I cannot separate "subagents do not inherit the env-file export" from the standing mk-i3u8 condition on this machine. The inheritance of the parent id and the presence of `CLAUDE_CODE_CHILD_SESSION=1` are unambiguous; the cause of the absence is not.
- **`f-031`'s 42% is an exposure estimate, not an observed alarm rate.** I derived it from commit timestamps: the interval between a root-touching non-bump commit and the next version bump, summed over 26.2 days. It assumes the cache is republished on version bumps and not otherwise, which the `installed_plugins.json` `lastUpdated` timestamp (76 s after the `0.6.303` bump) supports but does not prove. A daily check samples that exposure once per day, so the realized red-day rate could differ. The direction is not in doubt; the exact number is a first measurement, not a settled one.
- **Whether hook processes inherit `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` was never tested by anyone.** The plan marks it unverified and says the design does not depend on it because hooks read stdin first — but W2 routes the no-stdin library callers (`lib-sprint.sh`'s six sites, `dispatch.sh`, `lib-routing.sh`) through the env chain, and those run inside hook processes. If hooks do not inherit it, that chain collapses to `CLAUDE_SESSION_ID` alone, which is the original defect, in exactly the sessions the plan exists for. This is one `env` line inside a hook away from being answered and it is still open after three rounds and this synthesis.
- **W5's behavior is entirely unobserved.** `rig-clavain-deployed.py` does not exist. Every verdict about it, mine included, is read off prose. My dry-run of its comparison logic (0 STALE / 0 MISSING / 0 EXTRA, 0.08 s) implements the plan's step 4 as I read it, not as it will be written.

- **Zero fusion attempts by the loop.** Two productive pairs were available and unexploited. One I executed at synthesis time with the repos in hand; the other is reconstructed rather than investigated, so its evidence is thinner than a real fusion round's would be.
