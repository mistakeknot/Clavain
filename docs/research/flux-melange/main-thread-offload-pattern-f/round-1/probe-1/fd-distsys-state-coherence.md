## Findings

### validator-mechanism-misattributed-catch-rate
- Severity: P1
- Where: docs/brainstorms/2026-09-03-main-thread-offload-pattern-f.md:17,19,30 vs. commands/model-routing.md:99,114-128
- What: The prior finding asserts the validator's contract "is the exact configuration this repo's own pilot-1 evidence already measured at 0/6 gauge-defect catches." That figure is misattributed. Pilot-1's table row (model-routing.md:99) reports two *distinct* mechanisms with opposite results: `plan-gauge-lint.py` — mechanical execution of the verify block against a dry-run of the plan's own edits, diffed against the plan's separately-stated expectations — caught 6/6 ("the linter replays all 6"); a *reading*-based review by a model that never executes anything caught 0/6 ("reviewing caught none of them"). Rule 9's closing line makes the distinction explicit: "reading cannot see it and execution can" (model-routing.md:128). Pattern F's validator does not read — it "re-runs the verify block itself" (doc:17), which is an execution-type action, structurally the *closer* analog to the 6/6 mechanism, not the 0/6 one. So the literal claim "this exact configuration... measured at 0/6" is not supported by the cited evidence: pilot-1 never ran "re-execute the frozen verify block against the real post-execution tree, with no independent comparator" as a discrete, measured condition at all.
  That said, the underlying worry is not merely rescued but sharpened once the mechanisms are told apart correctly. Gauge-lint's 6/6 power comes from a specific ingredient the validator's contract omits: an *independent third artifact* to diff against (the plan's stated expectations, compared against a dry-run of the plan's own literal edits — plan-review.md:30-32, "applies the plan's own edits to a virtual copy of the tree... compares against the plan's stated expectations"). The validator has no such second source — only "the frozen criteria in the plan" (doc:17), which is the same self-authored verify block being re-run against its own prior output. A self-referential re-check of a check against itself cannot, by construction, surface a defect *in* that check — regardless of whether the check is executed once or twice. The validator's action superficially resembles gauge-lint's ("run the verify block") but is missing exactly the part that made gauge-lint work.
- Evidence: model-routing.md:99 ("Reviewing caught none of them across two rounds — the linter replays all 6"); model-routing.md:114-128, rule 9 ("Reading the gauge is not the control... It is n=6 that reading cannot see it and execution can"); plan-review.md:30-32 (gauge-lint's independent-comparator mechanism); doc:17 (validator: "Re-runs the verify block itself. Judges ONLY against the frozen criteria in the plan").
- Verdict: **PARTIALLY REFUTED, then sharpened.** The empirical citation (0/6) is misattributed to the wrong pilot-1 mechanism — it belongs to reading-review, not execution-based re-runs, and pilot-1 never measured the validator's actual configuration. But the corrected analysis lands on a stronger claim than the original: the validator's re-run is missing gauge-lint's independent-expectations comparator, so it is architecturally close to a null check for the exact defect class pilot-1 found dominant (6/6 verify-block-authoring defects), even though it superficially resembles the mechanism that worked.
- Suggestion: When restating this finding, drop the "0/6 configuration already measured" framing (unsupported) and replace it with "the validator's re-run lacks gauge-lint's independent stated-expectations comparator, so its catch rate for verify-block-authoring defects is unmeasured and structurally closer to zero than the executor's own identical run."
- Remediation: Amend Q-A to ask not just whether the validator adds information beyond the executor's identical run, but specifically whether the validator should re-run gauge-lint's independent-expectations comparison (not merely the self-referential frozen verify block) — the doc's current Q-A framing conflates "re-executing a check" with "independently checking the check," and pilot-1's own evidence (6/6 vs 0/6) shows only the latter caught anything.

### escalation-repair-skips-gauge-relint
- Severity: P1
- Where: docs/brainstorms/2026-09-03-main-thread-offload-pattern-f.md:19 vs. commands/model-routing.md:114-119 (rule 9) and commands/plan-review.md:14-26 (Step 0, gauge lint)
- What: Two-strikes escalation returns a failed item "to the orchestrator, which fixes the plan (usual case: the verify block was wrong, per pilot-1's 6/6 gauge defects)" (doc:19) and then, implicitly, redispatches it. Gauge-lint is wired as "step 0 of `/plan-review`" (plan-review.md:26) — the *initial* review flow before first freeze — and rule 9 describes it as a one-time, pre-freeze gate ("and only then reviewed by a model other than the author, and frozen," model-routing.md:117-119). Nothing in the design — not the doc's shape, not rule 9, not plan-review.md's Step 0 — names a re-entry hook that forces a *repaired* plan back through gauge-lint before it resets the two-strikes counter and goes to a fresh executor/validator pair. This is not a hypothetical gap: pilot-1's own round 2 struck out both arms again "on a third gauge defect each" after round-1 defects were repaired (model-routing.md:122-123) — meaning an unlinted repair reintroducing a fresh gauge defect is the literal failure mode this design already has one data point for, and Pattern F's escalation path has no stated mechanism to prevent a repeat.
- Evidence: doc:19; model-routing.md:114-119 (gauge-lint as pre-freeze, one-shot gate); model-routing.md:121-123 ("round 2 struck out both arms again, on a third gauge defect each"); plan-review.md:21-26 (Step 0 gauge lint framed as the entry gate to `/plan-review`, not to a repair-and-redispatch path).
- Suggestion: Require any orchestrator-authored plan repair issued after an escalation to re-run `plan-gauge-lint.py` before redispatch, and treat a repaired-but-unrelinted plan as ineligible to reset the two-strikes counter for that item.
- Verdict: Confirmed as a distinct, novel gap (not covered by the settled durable-strike-counter finding, which is about counter storage, not re-gating the repaired artifact itself).

### gauge-lint-certifies-hypothetical-not-real-diff
- Severity: P2
- Where: commands/plan-review.md:30-32 vs. docs/brainstorms/2026-09-03-main-thread-offload-pattern-f.md:16-17
- What: Gauge-lint's clean bill is computed against a *hypothetical* rendering — "applies the plan's own edits to a virtual copy of the tree" (plan-review.md:30) — i.e., a literal, mechanical application of exactly what the plan text specifies. The validator later judges the *real* executor's actual diff against the same frozen criteria (doc:17), but receives only "the same plan and the executor's report" (doc:17) — not a diff between the executor's real changes and the dry-run baseline gauge-lint certified. If the executor's real application deviates even slightly from the plan's literal edit set (a merge-conflict resolution, an adjacent one-line fix folded in without flagging, a partial application before stopping), gauge-lint's 6/6-proven guarantee — which was computed against the *hypothetical* diff, not this one — no longer covers the artifact under validation, and neither the executor's contract ("Applies the plan... commits," doc:16) nor the validator's ("Re-runs the verify block itself... against the frozen criteria," doc:17) includes a step that checks the real diff against the plan's specified edit set.
- Evidence: plan-review.md:30-32; doc:16 (executor role, no diff-against-plan-spec assertion stated); doc:17 (validator role, receives plan + report, not a diff-against-dry-run-baseline).
- Suggestion: Add a cheap mechanical pre-check (before or alongside the validator) that diffs the executor's actually-changed files against the plan's specified edit set, and treats any unexplained delta as an automatic defect-report regardless of verify-block PASS/FAIL.
- Verdict: Confirmed as a narrower, distinct gap from the other two — the window is smaller (executors are contractually barred from silent scope expansion) but unenforced, so it remains a live, if narrower, drift path.

## Verdict
The prior finding's headline evidentiary claim doesn't survive close reading — the 0/6 figure belongs to pilot-1's reading-based review, not to any execution-based re-run, and the validator's exact configuration was never separately measured. But re-deriving the mechanism correctly produces a stronger, more actionable claim: the validator is missing gauge-lint's independent stated-expectations comparator, making it architecturally close to a null check for the defect class pilot-1's own evidence says is dominant. Two further, non-overlapping gaps compound this: escalation repairs have no stated re-entry into the one mechanism proven to work (gauge-lint), and gauge-lint's certification is bound to a hypothetical dry-run diff that nothing re-confirms against the executor's real one.
