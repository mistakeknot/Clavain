## Findings

### mechanism-search-converted-to-word-search

- **Severity:** P1 (CONFIRMS the prior finding, with a stronger mechanism)
- **Where:** `docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-alternatives-charter.md:12-14`, `:49-61`, `:65-72`, `:74-83`, `:95-96`, `:111-119`
- **What:** The prior finding is confirmed on every element it names, and the confirmation is stronger than claimed. Line 68 mandates "at least one candidate inspired by ... control theory or cybernetics." But the primitives that class actually offers — a dead-band or hysteresis element on the Reflect→Compound promotion gate, a variety amplifier on Orient's input channel, a model-reference adaptive step, a change of loop gain or sampling rate on an existing edge — are **edge modifiers, not boxes**. They have no input/output contract, no re-entry path, and no "is it a phase or a side-loop" answer. Yet the per-candidate spec at `:74-83` demands exactly those four things of every serious candidate. So the control-theory requirement at `:68` is satisfiable only by something that impersonates a phase. The S-word seed list (`:53-61`, nine entries, eight of them S-verbs), the `Mnemonic/taste: concise, verb-shaped, pronounceable` criterion (`:95`), and the `Complexity cost: earns a new letter` criterion (`:96` — the unit of cost is literally a letter) then finish the conversion. Nothing in the required output (`:111-119`) asks which edges of the loop change, or what the mechanism would be called if the S constraint were dropped. Line 14 permits abandoning the letter but never requires the review to report what the letter cost it.
- **Evidence:** `:12` frames the decision question as "What ... should the optional **S** in OODARC(+S) mean?" — a word-slot question. `:68` demands a cybernetics candidate; `:76`/`:79`/`:83` demand of it a "Distinct input and output contract", a "Re-entry path into OODARC", and a phase/side-loop/tool/artifact classification. The runtime confirms the excluded class is the interesting one: the only cross-session learning edge is `internal/agent/agent.go:246-253` (Compound writes `QualitySignal`) → `internal/tool/builtin.go:27-28` (`quality_history` gated to `PhaseOrient` alone) → `internal/session/session.go:179` (`ReadRecent(5)`). That edge has exactly two tunable control parameters — the promotion gate and the 5-sample window — and neither can be proposed as a candidate under the current spec schema.
- **Suggestion:** Add one bullet to `:74-83`: "9. Loop delta — which edges of the O/O/D/A/R/C diagram are added, removed, or re-parameterised; a candidate whose deletion leaves the diagram unchanged is a capability, not a phase." Then add one seat to the longlist reserved for a non-S, non-box candidate (an edge modifier), exempt from `:95`.

### pilot-fitness-function-signs-against-exploration

- **Severity:** P1
- **Where:** `docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-alternatives-charter.md:119` (output item 7), `:93` (Generativity), `:96` (Complexity cost)
- **What:** The pilot's outcome measure in this runtime is `QualitySignal`, and `QualitySignal.Scores()` (`internal/mutations/signal.go:52-70`) is monotonically hostile to exploration: token efficiency (higher better), `-turn_count`, `-tool_error_rate`, `-tool_denial_rate`, approval rate, outcome. A Scout/Search/Speculate step spends tokens and turns in the cycle where it fires and pays off — if at all — in a later cycle, in a dimension (cross-domain transfer) that `Scores()` does not have. `internal/mutations/aggregate.go:63-70` computes `TokenEfficiency = totalOut/totalIn` over the whole session, so the scouting turns dilute it directly. Orient then reads a five-session moving average (`internal/session/session.go:179`), which is shorter than any plausible transfer payoff horizon, and `internal/mutations/best.go:9` Pareto-selects over the same one-sided vector. The tournament criteria have the mirror-image defect: `Generativity` (`:93`) rewards exploration with no criterion anywhere in `:89-96` pricing it, and `Complexity cost` (`:96`) counts letters, not turns. Net effect: the rhetoric is monotone pro-S and the only measurement is monotone anti-S, and neither is diagnostic.
- **Failure scenario:** The review shortlists Scout, runs the item-7 pilot for ~10 sessions. Every scouted session shows lower `TokenEfficiency` and higher `TurnCount`; no session shows a transfer benefit because no field records one. The pilot reports "Scout degrades quality", the team drops it — and would have reported the same for a Scout that worked perfectly. Conversely, if the team ignores the number because it "feels" generative, the pilot was never falsifiable.
- **Suggestion:** Make item 7 require, before any pilot runs, one new field the candidate writes and `Aggregate` carries — e.g. a `transfer_applied` counter incremented when a compounded lesson traces to a scout report — and state the pilot's decision rule as a comparison on that field, not on `TokenEfficiency`/`TurnCount`.
- **Remediation (brief amendment):** Require every pilot proposal to name the outcome field in `mutations.QualitySignal` that could move in the candidate's favour, and reject any pilot whose only measurable footprint is cost.

### pace-layer-criterion-has-no-runtime-referent

- **Severity:** P2
- **Where:** `docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-alternatives-charter.md:27`, `:78` (spec item 3), `:92` (Pace/shear fit)
- **What:** The charter asserts OODARC is "coupled loops at multiple pace layers rather than one mandatory linear turn sequence" and makes pace a first-class tournament criterion, but names no pace layer anywhere. The runtime carries exactly two cadences and neither is mentioned: the per-turn `agentloop` inside one phase (`internal/agent/agent.go:230-241`) and the per-session outer edge whose lag constant is the literal `5` in `ReadRecent(5)` (`internal/session/session.go:179`). `grep -ri "pace\|cadence"` over `internal/` returns no OODARC-related hit. The FSM itself is strictly linear and one-way (`internal/agent/phase.go:10-50`: fixed `phaseOrder`, `Advance()` only, `IsTerminal()` at Compound), so "coupled loops" describes an intention, not the artifact under review. Consequence for the tournament: every candidate will be scored on Pace/shear fit against the same undefined scale, so the criterion cannot separate any two candidates and degrades into a tiebreaker rather than the structural filter the charter wants it to be.
- **Suggestion:** In `:27`, name the layers that exist — per-turn (within-phase agentloop), per-session (Compound→Orient via `QualitySignal`, window 5) — and mark any third layer as aspirational; then restate `:92` as "states which of those two layers it fires on, and what happens if it fires on the other."

### recommendation-carries-no-expiry

- **Severity:** P2
- **Where:** `docs/research/2026-08-24-oodarcs-s-phase-alternatives-charter.md:117` (output item 5), against `:39`
- **What:** Scout reports are required to carry an "expiration or review condition" (`:39`), and item 5 requires "a recommendation with confidence and explicit conditions under which it changes." But the conditions are unconstrained: nothing requires them to be observable in an artifact the runtime emits, and nothing sets a revisit date or a cycle count. The charter therefore imposes a decay term on the speculative artifacts it governs and none on its own output, which is the higher-authority write. In control terms the review is an open-loop controller change with no feedback path back to itself.
- **Suggestion:** Extend item 5 to "...explicit conditions under which it changes, each stated as a predicate over a named field in `mutations.QualitySignal` or the evidence JSONL, plus a review-by cycle count."
- **Remediation (brief amendment):** Require the recommendation itself to carry the same expiration-or-review-condition field the charter demands of scout reports.

## Verdict

The prior finding is CONFIRMED and should be widened: the substitution is not only lexical — the per-candidate spec at `:74-83` is box-shaped, so the control-primitive class the charter itself mandates at `:68` (edge modifiers on the promotion gate and on Orient's input channel) cannot be entered as a candidate at all, and the substitution cost is nowhere required to be declared. The sharper new risk is that the charter's only empirical instrument is sign-locked against exploration: `QualitySignal.Scores()` has no dimension in which a scouting step can pay off within the five-session window Orient reads, so the item-7 pilot would report "S degrades quality" even for an S that worked.
